**1. Title & Course**

GreenGrid — B2B Carbon Footprint Optimization & Audit Tool
Environmental Studies (CHE110)

**2. Objective**

GreenGrid's objective is to provide small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) with a lightweight, accessible tool to measure, report, and reduce their daily operational carbon emissions. The emphasis on "lightweight" and "accessible" is deliberate — the tool is designed to work within the constraints SMEs actually face: no dedicated sustainability staff, no budget for enterprise carbon-accounting software, and no time to learn a complex new system. By keeping the entire tool inside a spreadsheet format that most business owners already use for their accounts or inventory, GreenGrid removes the learning curve almost entirely. The goal is not just to produce a number, but to create a repeatable monthly habit that turns emissions tracking from an occasional afterthought into routine business practice.

**3. Problem Statement**

Large corporations increasingly have sustainability departments, ESG reporting software, and consultants to manage their carbon footprint, largely because regulation and investor pressure require it of them. SMEs face no such pressure and possess none of those resources, yet collectively they represent a very large share of a local economy's total emissions simply because there are so many of them. This creates a measurement gap: the businesses least equipped to track their emissions are often the ones whose combined impact is most significant at a community level. Without a simple way to see where their emissions come from, SME owners have no basis for making informed decisions about where to cut costs and reduce environmental impact — the two goals are usually connected, since reducing energy and gas waste also reduces operating expense. GreenGrid is built specifically to close this gap, not by asking SMEs to adopt enterprise-grade software, but by meeting them where they already are: a spreadsheet.

**4. Target Audience**

The tool is aimed at campus eateries, retail stores, and small local offices. These three categories were chosen because they share a common emissions profile — their footprint is overwhelmingly driven by electricity use (lighting, refrigeration, computers), gas use (cooking, heating), and solid waste generation (packaging, food waste, general refuse) — the exact three inputs GreenGrid is built around. They also share a common constraint: limited staff time and no specialized environmental knowledge, meaning any tool aimed at them has to be usable without training.

**5. How It Works — Methodology**

GreenGrid operates on a simple four-stage logic, deliberately mirroring how a business owner already thinks about monthly operating costs:

*Stage 1 — Input:* Once a month, the owner or manager enters three figures they already have on hand from their utility bills and waste records — electricity used in kilowatt-hours (kWh), LPG or gas used in kilograms (kg), and solid waste generated in kilograms (kg). No new measurement equipment or process is required; these are numbers that already exist on an electricity bill or a gas cylinder receipt.

*Stage 2 — Convert:* GreenGrid applies standardized emissions factors to translate each raw usage figure into kilograms of carbon dioxide-equivalent (CO2e) — the common unit used to compare different emission sources on the same scale. Electricity is converted at approximately 0.82 kg CO2e per kWh, and LPG/gas at approximately 2.98 kg CO2e per kg. These factors are the accepted conversion standards used in emissions accounting, allowing very different activities (burning gas versus consuming grid electricity) to be added together meaningfully.

*Stage 3 — Report:* The tool totals these converted figures into a single Total Monthly Footprint, expressed in metric tons of CO2e, and breaks the total down by source so the business can see at a glance whether electricity, gas, or waste is driving most of their impact.

*Stage 4 — Act:* Rather than stopping at a number, GreenGrid automatically generates specific, prioritized recommendations based on that business's own results — for example, suggesting a transition to LED lighting for an estimated 12% reduction when electricity is the dominant source, recommending optimized cooling/HVAC schedules, or suggesting waste segregation when waste makes up a meaningful share of the total. This final step is what distinguishes GreenGrid from a simple calculator — it closes the loop between measurement and action.

**6. Deliverables in This Package**

The folder contains two core deliverables. README.md is this project overview, documenting the objective, problem, audience, and methodology. GreenGrid_Calculator.xlsx is the working product itself — a three-tab spreadsheet consisting of an Inputs tab for monthly data entry, an Emissions Factors tab documenting the conversion factors and their sources, and a Dashboard tab that auto-calculates the total footprint and generates the recommendations, so that no manual calculation is ever required by the end user.

**7. Why It Matters**

A tool that an SME can realistically use is a tool that actually gets used — a sophisticated carbon-accounting platform that requires training or subscription cost will simply be ignored by a business with neither the time nor budget for it. By keeping GreenGrid inside a familiar spreadsheet format with zero setup cost and zero learning curve, it removes the two most common barriers to SME-level carbon accounting: cost and complexity. This turns an intention that often gets postponed indefinitely — "we should probably start tracking our emissions" — into something that can be done in under ten minutes once a month, which is the difference between a good idea and a habit that actually reduces emissions over time.

**8. Future Extensions**

Planned extensions include a multi-location roll-up for businesses operating several branches, the addition of water usage and Scope 3 (supply chain) emissions categories, a simple web front-end built on the same calculation logic for businesses that prefer not to use a spreadsheet directly, and sector-average benchmarking so a business can see how their footprint compares to similar businesses in their category.
